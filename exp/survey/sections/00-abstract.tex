Formal results on the security of machine learning are hard to compare, because a
claim about an attack rarely states what the attacker may spend. We read
\NumSources{} formal works and record, for each one, which party's resources enter
its main claim, in what unit, and how tightly, with the sentence that decided each
classification quoted and checked against its source. \NumUnstated{} of the works
bound no party at all, and the works that study a named attack do so more often
than those drawn from cryptography and learning theory, by a margin this corpus
establishes in direction and not in size. Only \NumRelative{} works state a bound
as a relation between two parties' work, the form that lets two results share a
scale, and \NumRelativeAttack{} of those study an attack. Where the attack
literature does bound an adversary it usually bounds what the adversary may change,
since \AttackBudget{} of the \NumAttackWorks{} attack works state a numeric
allowance such as a perturbation radius. We identify \NearMiss{} works holding a
resource quantity their own framing does not carry, \NearMissAdv{} of them an
adversary's cost.
