Formal results on the security of machine learning are hard to compare, because a
claim about an attack rarely states what the attacker may spend. We read
\NumSources{} formal works and record, for each one, which party's resources enter
its main claim, in what unit, and how tightly, with the deciding sentence of each
source quoted and checked against it. \NumUnstated{} of the works bound no party at
all, and the works that study a named attack do so more often than those drawn from
cryptography and learning theory, by a margin this corpus establishes in direction
but not in size. Only \NumRelative{} works state a bound relative to another party's
work, the form that lets two results share a scale, and \NumRelativeAttack{} of
those study an attack. Where the attack literature does bound an adversary it
usually bounds what the adversary may change, since \AttackBudget{} of the
\NumAttackWorks{} attack works state a numeric allowance such as a perturbation
radius. We identify \NearMiss{} works holding a resource quantity their own framing
does not carry.
