\section{The literature by constrained party}
\label{sec:literature}

Section~\ref{sec:findings-tables} reports how the corpus distributes. This section
says what is in it. The order follows the party each region constrains, because
that is the axis on which the regions differ, and each subsection states what the
region contains, the shape its characteristic result takes, and the boundary that
shape imposes.

\subsection{The attacker bounded in time}

The largest region bounds an adversary in running time or circuit size, 34 works in
all. Its characteristic result is an impossibility proved against every efficient
algorithm.

Computational differential privacy is the definitional core. Mironov et al.\ relax
the differential privacy guarantee from every distinguisher to every efficient one,
producing the indistinguishability and simulation variants that later work takes as
given \cite{MPRV09}. What that relaxation buys is contested. Groce, Katz and
Yerukhimovich show it buys nothing in the single-curator setting, though their
result is narrower than it is usually quoted as being, applying to black-box
constructions and to queries whose output is a tuple of real numbers of constant
dimension \cite{GKY11}. The authors exclude, by name, queries returning
classifiers, graphs or synthetic databases, which is the case a released model
falls into. Bun, Chen and Vadhan separate the two notions infeasibly
\cite{BCV16}.

Backdoors give the region its sharpest results. Goldwasser, Kim, Vaikuntanathan and
Zamir plant a backdoor in a classifier so that no polynomial-time distinguisher with
oracle access can tell the backdoored model from an honestly trained one
\cite{GKVZ22}. The bound sits on the distinguisher, so the constrained party is
whoever audits rather than whoever attacks. Kalavasis et al.\ extend the guarantee
to white-box access under obfuscation, and note that the earlier work leaves that
case open \cite{KKOSVZ24}.

Robustness from hardness runs the implication the other way. Degwekar, Nakkiran and
Vaikuntanathan prove that a task where an efficient robust classifier exists but
cannot be learned efficiently implies one-way functions \cite{DNV19}. The direction
matters and is easy to invert. The theorem licenses no conclusion from the belief
that one-way functions exist, and what it supports is that a learning task with that
hardness profile carries cryptographic structure rather than arising by accident.

\subsection{The attacker bounded by what it may change}

The next largest region, 28 works, bounds what the adversary may alter and leaves
what it may spend open. Twenty-three fix a perturbation radius and five a fraction of the
training set.

Certified defences are the mature part. Cohen, Rosenfeld and Kolter certify a radius
around an input within which a smoothed classifier's prediction cannot change
\cite{CRK19}, and Steinhardt, Koh and Liang certify a bound on the loss a poisoning
adversary can induce while controlling a fixed fraction of the data \cite{SKL17}.
Both are guarantees against an adversary with unlimited computation and a limited
reach.

That combination is the region's defining feature and its boundary. A radius says
what an attack may do and nothing about what it costs, so two defences certified at
the same radius are indistinguishable under it even when one is far more expensive
to attack. Section~\ref{sec:findings-tables} records that this is the most common
form of adversary bound in the attack literature.

\subsection{The learner bounded}

Forty-five works constrain the party that trains rather than the party that attacks.
Seventeen bound it in time, thirteen in an algorithm class, ten in samples.

The algorithm-class results are the ones that transfer. Feldman et al.\ prove that
statistical-query algorithms need superpolynomially many queries to detect a planted
clique \cite{FGRVX17}, and the low-degree framework gives the same shape of bound
against polynomial thresholds \cite{Wei25,BBHLS21}. Because gradient descent and
principal component analysis are statistical-query algorithms, these bounds apply to
the methods learning actually uses. Bubeck, Price and Razenshteyn use exactly this
to separate robust from ordinary learning, and their separation rests on no
cryptographic assumption at all \cite{BPR19}.

Sample complexity is the second unit. Montasser, Hanneke and Srebro show that every
class of finite VC dimension can be learned under adversarial perturbation, though
not by empirical risk minimization and sometimes at a sample cost exponential in
the dimension
\cite{MHS19}, and later characterize the sample complexity of the agnostic case
\cite{MHS22}. Nothing in either is computational.

Two results bound the learner in a resource the rest of the corpus does not use.
Bubeck and Sellke show that smoothly interpolating $n$ points in $d$ dimensions
needs about $nd$ parameters, an information-theoretic law with no adversary in it
\cite{BS21}. Raz shows that learning parity from a stream needs either quadratic
memory or exponentially many samples \cite{Raz16}, which is the memory bound the
bounded-storage literature builds on \cite{GZ19}.

\subsection{Nobody bounded}

Thirty-two works constrain no party at all. They are not a residue, and two distinct
lines sit inside them.

The first concludes inevitability from geometry. Mahloujifar, Diochnos and Mahmoody
prove that in a concentrated metric probability space any classifier with constant
error is vulnerable to small perturbations \cite{MDM19}, and Gilmer et al.\ exhibit
the effect on a constructed distribution of two concentric spheres
\cite{GMFSRWG18}. These conclude inevitability precisely because no resource enters
them, which is why the frame of this survey does not hold them and why the survey
says so.

The hypothesis those theorems rest on has been measured. Mahloujifar, Zhang,
Mahmoody and Evans estimate the concentration function on image benchmarks and find
it does not explain observed vulnerability \cite{MZME19}. A conditional theorem
whose antecedent is measured false is a limit on the theorem, not a sibling result,
and the corpus records it as one.

The second line is critique of evaluation. Athalye et al.\ and Tram\`er et al.\
break defences inside their own threat models \cite{ACW18,TCBM20}, Das et al.\ show
that classifiers which never query a model beat published membership inference
attacks on eight benchmarks \cite{DZT25}, and Arp et al.\ measure the prevalence of
ten pitfalls across four venues \cite{AQPWPWCR22}. These works bound nobody because
their claims are about practice.

\subsection{The auditor and the mechanism}

Nineteen works bound a party that is neither attacking nor learning. Twelve bound an
auditor, seven a mechanism.

The auditor results are impossibilities about detection. Ball et al.\ show there
exist language models for which no efficient prompt filter works, under a
construction in which the model shares a secret key with the attacker
\cite{BGGKRR25}. The claim is existential and about constructed models, and it has
repeatedly been restated as a universal claim about deployed ones.

The mechanism results price the defence itself. Dinur et al.\ bound the space a
differentially private mechanism may use while remaining sound under adaptive
queries \cite{DSWZ23}, and Ratliff and Vadhan treat the mechanism's own running time
as a leakage channel \cite{RV24}. Both put the resource bound on the system rather
than on anyone attacking it.

\subsection{Where the two halves meet, and do not}

Fine-grained cryptography is the one region that states a bound as a relation
between two parties' work \cite{BC22,LLW19,BRSV17,DVV16}. It contains no machine
learning. The proof-of-learning line states the same relation informally, requiring
that forging a training record cost at least as much as training
\cite{JYCDTCP21}, and its own authors could not prove it, a gap their follow-up
confirms \cite{FJTYCDCP23}.

The learning-theory exponents are the closest the machine learning side comes to a
priced adversary. The information exponent \cite{AGJ21}, the leap \cite{ABM23} and
the generative exponent \cite{DPLB24,DLB25} each give a sample threshold defined
against an algorithm class.

Two boundaries on those thresholds are quoted more often than they now hold. The
first is that the thresholds measure detection rather than recovery, which the
single-index result states of itself \cite{DPLB24}. That gap has since been closed
for polynomial links. Arnaboldi et al.\ carry weak recovery through to strong
recovery and a population error bound at $\tilde\Theta(d^{(p^*-1) \vee 1})$ samples,
governed by the generative exponent \cite{LOSW24}, and Glasgow reaches $o(1)$
population error on the Boolean parity instance in $\tilde\Theta(d)$ \cite{Gla24}.
An adversary that must recover rather than detect now has a threshold to be priced
against, at least for these targets.

The second is that the exponents price single-pass training, since gradient descent
that reuses batches was reported to escape them \cite{DTAPZK24}. Escape is the
wrong word for what reuse does. Two steps on one minibatch pass the label through
the activation's derivative, which is a transformation a correlational
statistical-query learner cannot make, and the effect is to attain the generative
exponent in place of the information exponent rather than to leave the family
\cite{LOSW24}. The learning rate selects between the two regimes \cite{TME25}. The
exponents are therefore narrower than a security claim would want and less fragile
than the escape reading suggests.

One work crosses in the other direction. Haghifam, Smith and Ullman show a setting
where a membership inference attacker needs many more samples than the training
algorithm used \cite{HSU25}. The separation vanishes when the attacker knows the
covariance, so what it prices is ignorance of the data distribution rather than
attack difficulty as such.
