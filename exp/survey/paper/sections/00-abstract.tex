Security claims about machine learning are hard to compare, because a claim
about an attack rarely states what the attacker may spend. We read
\NumSources{} formal works on the security and privacy of learning and record,
for each one, which party's resources enter its main claim, in what unit, and
how tightly. We quote the deciding sentence from each work and check it
against the source document. \NumUnstated{} of the works bound no party at
all. Works studying a named attack bound nobody more often than works drawn
from cryptography and learning theory, and this corpus establishes that
difference in direction and not in size. Only \NumRelative{} works state a
bound as a relation between two parties' work, the one form that places two
results on a single scale, and \NumRelativeAttack{} of those study an attack.
Where the attack literature does bound an adversary it usually bounds what the
adversary may change, since \AttackBudget{} of the \NumAttackWorks{} attack
works state a numeric allowance such as a perturbation radius. We identify
\NearMiss{} works holding a resource quantity their own framing does not carry,
\NearMissAdv{} of them an adversary's cost.
