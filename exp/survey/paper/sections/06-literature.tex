\section{The literature by constrained party}
\label{sec:literature}

Section~\ref{sec:findings-tables} reports how the corpus distributes. This
section says what is in it. We order the regions by the party each one
constrains, because that is the axis on which they differ, and each subsection
states what the region contains, the shape its characteristic result takes,
and the boundary that shape imposes.

\subsection{The attacker bounded in time}

The largest region bounds an adversary in running time or circuit size,
\AttackerTimeCirc{} works in all. Its characteristic result guarantees
something against every efficient algorithm and against no unbounded one.

Computational differential privacy is the definitional core. Mironov et al.\
relax the differential privacy guarantee from every distinguisher to every
efficient one, producing the indistinguishability and simulation variants that
later work takes as given \cite{MPRV09}. Authors disagree about what that
relaxation gains. Groce, Katz and Yerukhimovich show it gains nothing in the
single-curator setting, under a result narrower than its usual restatement,
applying to black-box constructions and to queries whose output is a tuple of
real numbers of constant dimension \cite{GKY11}. The authors exclude, by name,
queries returning classifiers, graphs or synthetic databases, which is the
case a released model falls into.

Robustness against a bounded attacker is the region's constructive side. Garg,
Jha, Mahloujifar and Mahmoody wrap a learning problem whose classifier yields
to every perturbation into one whose classifier resists polynomial-time
adversaries and still yields to unbounded ones, using signatures and
error-correcting codes
\cite{GJMM20}. Etesami, Mahloujifar and Mahmoody give a polynomial-time
algorithm that finds, on product distributions under Hamming distance, the
close points the concentration-of-measure bound promises, so an efficient
attacker attains the geometric inevitability of Section~\ref{sec:lit-none}
\cite{EMM20}. Cohen et al.\ define a computational form of reconstruction
resilience against polynomial-time attackers and show that, for training maps
that are efficiently computable and well-spread, it is equivalent to
one-wayness
\cite{CKMMNST25}.

\subsection{The attacker bounded by what it may change}

The next region, \AttackerCapability{} works, bounds what the adversary may
alter and leaves what it may spend open. \AttackerPerturbAll{} fix a
perturbation radius and \AttackerCorruptAll{} a fraction of the training set.

Certified defences are the mature part. Cohen, Rosenfeld and Kolter certify a
radius around an input within which a smoothed classifier's prediction cannot
change \cite{CRK19}, and Steinhardt, Koh and Liang certify a bound on the loss
a poisoning adversary can induce while controlling a fixed fraction of the
data
\cite{SKL17}. Both guarantee something against an adversary with unlimited
computation and a limited reach. The evaluation critiques fall in this region
too. Athalye et al.\ and Tram\`er et al.\ break defences inside their own
threat models, and the threat model each defence stated fixes a radius and
nothing else
\cite{ACW18,TCBM20}.

The combination of unlimited computation and a limited reach is the region's
defining feature and its boundary. A radius says what an attack may do and
nothing about what it costs, so two defences certified at the same radius are
indistinguishable under it even when one costs far more to attack.
Section~\ref{sec:findings-tables} records this as the most common form of
adversary bound in the attack literature.

\subsection{The learner bounded}

\LearnerBound{} works constrain the party that trains and not the party that
attacks. \LearnerTime{} bound it in time, \LearnerSamples{} in samples,
\LearnerClass{} in an algorithm class, \LearnerFlops{} in a budget of
floating-point operations, and \LearnerOther{} in parameters, circuit size,
memory or queries.

The algorithm-class results transfer furthest. Feldman et al.\ prove that
statistical-query algorithms need superpolynomially many queries to detect a
planted clique \cite{FGRVX17}, and the low-degree framework gives the same
shape of bound against polynomial thresholds \cite{Wei25,BBHLS21}. Gradient
descent and principal component analysis are statistical-query algorithms, so
these bounds apply to the methods learning actually uses. Bubeck, Price and
Razenshteyn use exactly this to separate robust from ordinary learning, and
their separation rests on no cryptographic assumption \cite{BPR19}.

The time-bounded results include the one that runs the implication from
robustness to cryptography. Degwekar, Nakkiran and Vaikuntanathan prove that a
task where an efficient robust classifier exists but cannot be learned
efficiently implies one-way functions \cite{DNV19}. The direction matters and
inverts easily. The theorem licenses no conclusion from the belief that
one-way functions exist. It supports the reading that a learning task with
that hardness profile carries cryptographic structure and does not arise by
accident.

Sample complexity is the second unit. Montasser, Hanneke and Srebro show that
every class of finite VC dimension can be learned under adversarial
perturbation, though not by empirical risk minimization and sometimes at a
sample cost exponential in the VC dimension \cite{MHS19}. They later
characterize the sample complexity of the agnostic case \cite{MHS22}. Nothing
in either is computational.

Two results bound the learner in a resource the rest of the corpus does not
use. Bubeck and Sellke show that smoothly interpolating $n$ points in $d$
dimensions needs about $nd$ parameters, an information-theoretic law with no
adversary in it \cite{BS21}. Raz shows that learning parity from a stream
needs either quadratic memory or exponentially many samples \cite{Raz16},
which is the memory bound the bounded-storage literature builds on
\cite{GZ19}.

\subsection{Nobody bounded}
\label{sec:lit-none}

\NumUnstated{} works constrain no party at all. They are two distinct lines and
not a residue.

The first concludes inevitability from geometry. Mahloujifar, Diochnos and
Mahmoody prove that in a concentrated metric probability space any classifier
with constant error is vulnerable to small perturbations \cite{MDM19}, and
Gilmer et al.\ exhibit the effect on a constructed distribution of two
concentric spheres \cite{GMFSRWG18}. The radius in each theorem is its
conclusion and not a restriction on any adversary. These works conclude
inevitability precisely because no resource enters them, so the frame of this
survey does not hold them, and we say so.

The hypothesis those theorems rest on has since been measured. Mahloujifar,
Zhang, Mahmoody and Evans estimate the concentration function on image
benchmarks and find that it does not explain observed vulnerability
\cite{MZME19}. A conditional theorem whose antecedent is measured false is a
limit on the theorem and not a sibling result, and the corpus records it as
one.

The second line criticizes evaluation and definition. Das et al.\ show that
classifiers which never query a model beat published membership inference
attacks on eight benchmarks \cite{DZT25}, Arp et al.\ measure the prevalence
of ten pitfalls across four venues \cite{AQPWPWCR22}, and Shafran et al.\
report that model extraction has no formal definition of success
\cite{SSEP24}. Balle, Cherubin and Hayes define reconstruction against an
informed adversary and quantify over every reconstruction attack with no bound
on it \cite{BCH22}, which is the definitional form the critiques ask for, and
it prices nobody. These works bound nobody because their claims concern
practice, or what success means, and not an adversary's cost.

\subsection{The auditor and the mechanism}

\AuditorBound{} works bound an auditor and \MechanismBound{} a mechanism,
parties that neither attack nor learn.

The auditor results are impossibilities about detection. Goldwasser, Kim,
Vaikuntanathan and Zamir plant a backdoor in a classifier so that no
polynomial-time distinguisher with oracle access can tell the backdoored model
from an honestly trained one \cite{GKVZ22}. The bound falls on the
distinguisher, so the constrained party is whoever audits. Kalavasis et al.\
extend the guarantee to white-box access under obfuscation, and note that the
earlier work leaves that case open \cite{KKOSVZ24}. Ball et al.\ show there
exist language models for which no efficient prompt filter works, under a
construction in which the model shares a secret key with the attacker
\cite{BGGKRR26}. Their claim is existential and concerns constructed models.

The mechanism results price the defence itself. Bun, Chen and Vadhan separate
computational from statistical differential privacy by exhibiting a
polynomial-time mechanism, so the bound falls on the mechanism \cite{BCV16}.
Dinur et al.\ bound the space a differentially private mechanism may use while
remaining sound under adaptive queries \cite{DSWZ23}, and Ratliff and Vadhan
treat the mechanism's own running time as a leakage channel \cite{RV24}. All
three put the resource bound on the system and not on anyone attacking it.

\subsection{Where the two halves meet, and do not}

Fine-grained cryptography is where the relation between two parties' work has
a name and a parameter \cite{BC22,LLW19,BRSV17}, and Degwekar, Vaikuntanathan
and Vasudevan restrict both parties to a circuit class instead \cite{DVV16}.
It contains no machine learning. The proof-of-learning line states the same
relation with the exponent fixed at one, as a requirement its authors could
not prove \cite{JYCDTCP21}, a gap their follow-up confirms \cite{FJTYCDCP23}.
The
\NumRelativeAttack{} attack works of Table~\ref{tab:relative} each state such a
relation, and none writes it in the fine-grained vocabulary or cites it.
% claim: two-party-bounds-rare-in-attack-papers
% claim: attacker-to-honest-ratio-defined-twice

The learning-theory exponents are the closest the machine learning side comes
to a priced adversary. The information exponent \cite{AGJ21}, the leap
\cite{ABM23} and the generative exponent \cite{DPLB24,DLB25} each give a sample
threshold defined against an algorithm class. Two boundaries on those
thresholds are quoted more often than they now hold. The thresholds were
stated for detection and not recovery, as the single-index result says of
itself
\cite{DPLB24}, and Lee, Oko, Suzuki and Wu carry weak recovery through to strong
recovery for polynomial links \cite{LOSW24,Gla24}. They were also stated for
single-pass training, and batch reuse attains the generative exponent in place
of the information exponent \cite{DTAPZK24,TME25,KJWSM26}. A security claim
resting on either boundary has to say which version it means.

\subsection{Training compute as a priced resource}

The corpus holds \NumScaling{} works on neural scaling laws, and \NumFlops{}
of them price a budget of floating-point operations spent on training. The
rest price the number of samples or of parameters a fixed budget affords.
Kaplan et al.\ fit loss against model size, data and compute
\cite{KMHBCCGRWA20}, and Hoffmann et al.\ correct the allocation between model
size and data at a fixed budget
\cite{HBMBCRCHWCHNMDDGOSERVS22}. Ho et al.\ measure how much of the observed
improvement comes from better algorithms and how much from more compute
\cite{HBEORGATS24}.

None of these works concerns an adversary, and all of them bound the trainer.
They matter here because they establish that the unit exists and that the
field measures it. Shavit's compute-monitoring proposal is the one work in the
corpus carrying a training-compute budget toward a security claim, and it
states a hardness requirement with no construction \cite{Sha23}. Its monitored
quantity is chip-hours and not operations, a further step from the unit the
scaling literature measures. One literature measures training compute
precisely, and the security literature states no adversary's budget in it.
That distance is the widest gap this corpus contains.

One work crosses in the other direction. Haghifam, Smith and Ullman show a
setting where a membership inference attacker needs many more samples than the
training algorithm used \cite{HSU25}. The separation vanishes when the
attacker knows the covariance, so what it prices is ignorance of the data
distribution and not attack difficulty as such.
