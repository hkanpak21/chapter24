\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A security claim about machine learning is comparable with another when both say
what their adversary may spend. Across \NumSources{} formal works,
\NumUnstated{} state no budget for any party. Among the \NumAttackWorks{} that
study a named attack \AttackUnbound{} do, a higher rate than among the works
drawn from cryptography and learning theory, and this corpus establishes that
difference in direction and not in size.
% claim: most-formal-works-price-nobody
% claim: attack-works-price-nobody-more-often
% claim: margin-direction-only

Where the attack literature does bound an adversary, it usually bounds what the
adversary may change and not what it must spend. \AttackBudget{} of the
\NumAttackWorks{} attack works state a numeric allowance such as a perturbation
radius. \NumRelativeAttack{} state their bound as a relation to another party's
work, the form that places two results on one scale.
% claim: attack-bounds-limit-change-not-spend
% claim: two-party-bounds-rare-in-attack-papers

The vocabulary for the missing form exists in fine-grained cryptography, and no
work in the machine learning half of this corpus uses it. The same holds for
adversaries bounded in memory, where two decades of cryptographic work has
produced no instance of an adversary attacking a learned model under a space
bound.
% claim: no-memory-bounded-adversary-attacks-a-model
