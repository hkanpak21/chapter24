### halil-2026-08-22-unbacked-superlative

The unbacked superlative

**From:** "Membership inference is the most studied attack on a trained model. The
most studied question about it is whether it works."

**To:** "Membership inference decides whether one record was in a model's training
set. Meeus et al. count at least ten new methods for it proposed in 2024 alone."

**Reason:** "most studied" is a superlative with no count behind it. The advisor's
checklist bans "most" without backing by name and `lint.py` flags it as `unbacked`.
Halil's objection was "who says". The repair is a counted fact with its citation,
not a softer superlative.

### halil-2026-08-22-citation-weight-in-the-gap-paragraph

Citation weight in the introduction's gap paragraph

**From:** the gap was carried by Fenaux and Kerschbaum, quoted first and at length.

**To:** the paragraph leads with Papernot et al. (IEEE EuroS&P) and Arp et al.

## Standing rules

### halil-2026-08-22-citation-hierarchy

Citation hierarchy, set 2026-08-22

When two sources support the same point, the one cited first and quoted at length
is chosen in this order.

1. A peer-reviewed paper at a top venue in the relevant field. For cryptography and
   security that is CRYPTO, EUROCRYPT, ASIACRYPT, TCC, IEEE S&P, USENIX Security,
   ACM CCS and NDSS. For theory it is STOC, FOCS, SODA, COLT and the corresponding
   journals. For machine learning it is NeurIPS, ICML and ICLR.
2. A peer-reviewed paper at any other venue, including workshops, PoPETs, SaTML and
   EuroS&P.
3. A preprint, or a paper whose venue this corpus could not confirm from the PDF.

A tier-3 source may still be cited. It may not carry an argument alone when a
higher-tier source says the same thing, and where it is load-bearing the text says
that it is a preprint. `tools/cite-weight.py` reports the tier of every citation in
a section, so this rule is checked rather than remembered.

### halil-2026-08-22-recency-is-checked-not-assumed

Recency, set 2026-08-22

This field moves fast enough that a result from two years ago may already have been
abandoned. When a verdict rests on a result, prefer the most recent work that
addresses it, and check whether a later paper has measured the hypothesis or
refuted the claim before repeating it. A citation from 2024 is not current by
default. The repository's own record of refuted claims is in the root `CLAUDE.md`
and is checked before any novelty or hardness claim goes into the paper.

### halil-2026-08-22-author-standing-is-a-tiebreaker

Author standing is a tiebreaker, not evidence

Halil also asked for weight on well-established researchers. Applied narrowly this
is sound, because a group that built a line of work usually states its boundaries
accurately. Applied broadly it substitutes reputation for evidence, and this corpus
has already recorded cases where a well-cited paper's headline overstated its own
theorem. Author standing therefore breaks a tie between two sources of the same
tier and the same recency, and it never outranks S1 or S2.

| Key | Resolved to | Evidence |

Section~2 went from four tier-3 citations to one. The three places where a tier-3
source still carries an argument now say so in the text: the three subfield
systematizations in the introduction, Zhang et al.\ in the problem section, and
Carlini et al.\ in Section 7.

**Reason:** Halil asked for a citation hierarchy weighted towards published work.
Applying it revealed that most of the apparent weakness was missing metadata rather
than weak sources. The technique generalises and should be used for the remaining
90 unconfirmed venues, since a venue can often be recovered from the corpus itself
with no web access.

### halil-2026-08-22-the-hierarchy-is-now-mechanical

The hierarchy is now mechanical

`tools/cite-weight.py` reports the tier and the age of every citation in a section,
so S1 and S2 are checked rather than remembered. Run it on any section before
handing that section over.

```
python3 tools/cite-weight.py sections/01-introduction.tex
python3 tools/cite-weight.py --all
```

Three bugs in its first version are worth recording, because each produced a wrong
tier. The bibliography's last field carries no trailing newline, so `annote` never
parsed and every entry looked confirmed. `IEEE S&P` is written `IEEE S\&P` in
BibTeX, so the top-venue pattern missed it. And a workshop at a top venue is not
the top venue, which the first version scored as tier 1.

### halil-2026-08-22-membership-inference-is-not-the-running-example

Membership inference is not the survey's running example

**From:** "Membership inference is the clearest case, and we use it throughout this
survey."

**To:** "Membership inference makes the defect concrete, and we use it to state the
three problems in Section 2", followed by an explicit statement of the corpus
breadth and the sentence "No single attack runs through every part of the survey,
so each part of Section 4 carries its own worked case."

**Reason:** Halil pointed out that the survey is not constrained to membership
inference. Counting settled it. Membership inference appears in **21 of the 181**
source pages, about 12 per cent. Evasion appears in 25, differential privacy in 25,
poisoning in 17, statistical-query in 16, low-degree in 14, fine-grained
cryptography in 14, planted clique in 11, backdoors in 10. Part III's six
subsections run from concentration of measure through backdoors, computational
differential privacy, statistical-query and low-degree hardness, fine-grained
cryptography, bounded memory and the learning exponents, and membership inference
is the subject of none of them.

**A claim of mine that was wrong, recorded so it is not repeated.** When
recommending the running example I wrote that membership inference "appears in five
of the six subsections of Part III". That was not checked against the corpus and it
is false. The correct figure is zero of six. This is the failure mode the repository
exists to prevent, an unverified quantitative claim stated with confidence, and it
survived two turns because nothing forced it through a count.

**What replaces the running example.** `references/checklist-paper.md` asks for
"a consistent paper-long running example **if one exists**". For a corpus spanning
attacks on models, attacks on training data, and cryptography with no machine
learning content at all, none exists. Examples are therefore used per section,
which is what the same checklist item asks for first.
