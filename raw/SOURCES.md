> Boundary note: `PSV23` uses "concentration" in the **opposite** geometric sense to `MDM19` /
> `GMF+18` / `FFF18`. There it means mass on a small-volume, low-dimensional set, and it makes robust

> concentration function, and it makes them unattainable. Same word, opposite property.

# Wave 2 — the adversary-resource corpus (ingested 2026-08-20)

Collected by three sweeps against the survey's implicit-quantity register. The organising question is
**what an adversary can be bounded by, and in what units**. Boundary notes are recorded here because
they are the reason each paper was taken; do not cite a row without reading the paper.

## Fine-grained cryptography — the adversary priced against honest work

| File | Title | Authors | Year | Boundary note |
|------|-------|---------|------|---------------|
| `BC22_FineGrainedOWF.pdf` | On Building Fine-Grained One-Way Functions from Strong Average-Case Hardness | Brzuska, Couteau | EUROCRYPT'22 / JoC'25 | Def 6 is the `honest^{1+δ}` convention. Adversary class is **non-uniform circuits** sized against `\|f\|`, not runtime. Predominantly a **negative** paper; the one construction needs "block finding hardness", non-standard |
| `LLV19_PKCFineGrained.pdf` | Public-Key Cryptography in the Fine-Grained Setting | LaVigne, Lincoln, Vassilevska Williams | CRYPTO'19 (per KPR25 bibliography) | Def 5 names **"the gap"**. Honest is `T^{1−δ}`, adversary gets `T`, in **uniform word-RAM time**. **Conversion, verified:** `δ_BC = δ_LLV/(1−δ_LLV)`; a quadratic gap is `δ=1` in BC22 and `δ=1/2` here. Even at matched numeric gap the two price different resources (circuit size vs time) in different machine models. The gap is also capped strictly below quadratic (`N^{2−4/(k+2)}`) |
| `DVV16_FineGrainedCrypto.pdf` | Fine-Grained Cryptography | Degwekar, Vaikuntanathan, Vasudevan | CRYPTO'16 | Adversary bounded by **circuit class**. Unconditional PRG/wPRF/SKE/CRHF against all of `AC0`. **Two caveats added 2026-08-20:** the `NC1`-side primitives are **infinitely-often** only (Remark 3.1, verified verbatim), and the abstract states the hypothesis as `NC1 ≠ ⊕L/poly` while every formal theorem needs `⊕L/poly ⊄ NC1` — not the same statement |
| `BGHKP24_PermissionlessConsensusFG.pdf` | Towards Permissionless Consensus in the Standard Model via Fine-Grained Complexity | Ball, Garay, Hall, Kiayias, Panagiotakos | CRYPTO'24 | The exponent-form ratio inside a security theorem: adversary controls `A = T^{1−ε}` of honest power `T`. **Title overreaches** — it is the random beacon model. Note the discrepancy is title-vs-theorem, not abstract-vs-theorem: the abstract already says "random beacon model", and the conclusion uses "standard model" to mean "no random oracle". Assumes worst-case OV/SETH |
| `ACMS23_FineGrainedNIKE.pdf` | Fine-Grained NIKE: Constructions and Lower Bounds | Afshar, Couteau, Mahmoody, Sadeghi | EUROCRYPT'23 | Ceiling result. In the ROM the gap is `n^{K/(K−1)}`, so it **shrinks as K grows**, not quadratic in general. The `O(n²)` cap is specifically 3-NIKE in **Maurer's** GGM. All query complexity, not time |
| `BCS24_FineGrainedNIKERevisited.pdf` | Fine-Grained NIKE, Revisited | Bauer, Couteau, Sadeghi | CRYPTO'24 | 4-party. Quadratic gap in the standard model from exponentially secure injective PRGs + subexponential CDH; only `N^{1.6}` in Maurer's GGM. Gap to `N²` still open |
| `WSPX24_NIKEWithoutIdealized.pdf` | Fine-Grained NIKE without Idealized Assumptions, and Lower Bounds | Wang, Su, Pan, Xu | CRYPTO'24 (stated in PDF) | Bounded parallel-time / bounded time / bounded storage models. **CORRECTED 2026-08-20:** an earlier note here said it states no quantitative ratio; that is false. Two of the three constructions state one — honest `Õ(λ^{n_p+k−1})` vs adversary `Õ(λ^{n_p+k})`, and user memory `O(λ^{n_p})` vs adversary `O(λ^{n_p+1})`. The defensible point is narrower: these are two **absolute exponents**, never a δ-indexed family, and the gap exponent is derived from the party count rather than chosen. Only the `AC0[2]`-vs-`NC1` construction has no ratio |
| `GM24_SequentialFunctions.pdf` | On Sequential Functions and Fine-Grained Cryptography | Guan, Montgomery | CRYPTO'24 | Priced resource is **depth/sequentiality**, not total work. The one compute axis crypto has fully formalized, and not the axis ML attack cost lives on. PKE needs VDF + iO for null circuits |
| `AHY25_FineGrainedNoCrypto.pdf` | Fine-Grained Complexity in a World without Cryptography | Alman, Huang, Yeo | EUROCRYPT'25 | Barrier. If OWFs do not exist, average-case k-SUM and Zero-k-Clique are false **for large constant k** — not all k, not worst-case, and **for infinitely many `n` only** |
| `DLSV25_AvgCaseParityProblems.pdf` | Average-Case Hardness of Parity Problems: OV, k-SUM and More | Dalirrooyfard, Lincoln, Saha, Vassilevska Williams | venue unconfirmed from PDF | **Parity/counting variants** on constructed distributions, not search or decision. Holds only for algorithms with success probability `1 − Θ(2^{-K})`; exponent degrades to `√K` / `K^{1/3}`. **CORRECTED:** it does not *claim* no cryptographic applications — it does not mention cryptography at all outside one bibliography entry |
| `KPR25_PlantedOrthogonalVectors.pdf` | The Planted Orthogonal Vectors Problem | Kühnemann, Polak, Rosen | 2025 | Average-case hardness of the planted instances is **conjectured, not proved** |

## Corrections filed 2026-08-20 (post-ingest verification)

- `DQW23_SpeakMuchRememberLittle.pdf` is dated **22 September 2021** on the PDF and is cited as `[DQW21]` inside `DQW22`; the EUROCRYPT'23 attribution is not stated in this copy. Its price: rounds `Õ(⌈m/n²⌉·λ)`, communication `Õ(m⌈m/n²⌉·λ)`, against lower bounds `R ≥ Ω(√m/n)` and `C ≥ Ω(m^{3/2}/n)`.
- `GZ19_BoundedStorageSimple.pdf` inherits Raz's exact constant, `C < 1/20`, not merely the shape. Its OT is indistinguishability-based, not simulation-based; binding is `2^{-λ}` from a hidden matrix, not from a storage bound. The PDF states no venue and no date.
- `BCEQ24_BSMKeyAgreementOWF.pdf` belongs in the memory section above: it is the only source in the corpus that prices a space bound **against** a computational assumption and proves the exchange runs both ways. Tight only for single-long-stream protocols (Thm 3); Thm 4 needs a much larger space gap and yields only non-uniform, **infinitely-often** one-way functions.
- `CCG+20_ActiveLearningExtraction.pdf`: the reduction is an **Observation** (Obs. 1), not a theorem, and Definition 3 lower-bounds one adversary's success by `γ` rather than upper-bounding all adversaries. There is no security clause to prove hard.
- `HKK+26_RigorousImplicationsLD.pdf`: **not** every statement assumes permutation invariance. Theorem 1.2 (Gaussian, symmetric polynomials) assumes only that `P` is a distribution over `R^n`; the test class supplies the symmetry. The **noise** condition is what is universal across all three results.
- `BHJK25_QuasiPolyLowDegreeFalse.pdf`: the **polynomial-time** distinguisher is for the rectangular generalisation (Thm 2.4 / Remark 2.3). In the square `S_n`-symmetric regime the conjecture was stated in, the distinguisher is `n^{O(log^{1/(k−1)} n)}` and improving it is open. The largest-eigenvalue construction has a non-product null and does not refute the conjecture.
